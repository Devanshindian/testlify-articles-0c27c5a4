Video Interview Software That Stops Deepfakes and Proxies

3,369 words · 13 sections · 4 sources

You are about to hire a backend engineer you have never met in person. The resume is strong. The recorded answers are fluent. On day one, you hand this person production database credentials. Nothing in your process has confirmed that the face in the video belongs to the applicant who signed the offer.

Published rankings of video interview platforms score workflow, analytics and ATS integrations. Almost none of them ask whether the person on camera is the candidate who applied. This comparison scores nine platforms on that question alone: ID and face match, proctoring and browser lockdown, and deepfake or proxy detection in recorded video.

TL;DR

  • No platform here covers all three integrity checks, so shortlist on the gap your riskiest open role can least afford to leave open.
  • VidCruiter rates highest because its fraud detection covers pre-recorded answers, which is where a stand-in is cheapest to send. Staff your live final rounds with trained interviewers.
  • Talview documents the deepest identity checks in the set, but it never states whether they run on one-way interviews, so get that coverage in writing before you sign.
  • Consent, bias audits and recording retention remain your legal duty, whatever certifications the vendor holds.

What Video Interview Software Is, and How We Scored It

Start with what you are comparing. Video interview software is a tool an employer, recruiter or hiring manager buys to collect and score candidate interviews. Vendors price it per month, per interview, or by organization size. A general video conferencing account does not qualify. Impersonation only gets harder when verification, monitoring and analysis sit behind the call.

Two formats create two different problems. A one-way interview is a file the candidate records alone and submits. A live interview is a real-time conversation with an interviewer on the other end. The recorded stage carries the largest screening volume, and nobody watches it happen. That makes it the cheapest place in your process to send a stand-in. On a live call, the detection mechanism is mostly the interviewer, who catches some impersonators and misses the practiced ones. Instrumentation belongs on the recorded stage, and trained observation on the live one.

Keep the fraud premise in proportion while you shop. Gartner projects that by 2028, one in four candidate profiles worldwide could be fake 1. The same firm's survey evidence is smaller: 6% of 3,000 candidates admitted posing as someone else, or having someone pose for them 1. Buy against that second number, and against the access your riskiest role carries. Do not buy against the headline.

Every rating below comes from the vendor's own security page, help center and product documentation rather than from marketing claims. Where a platform says nothing on an axis, its row says nothing. That silence is itself the finding, because a buyer defending this selection process a year later has nothing to cite.

VidCruiter: Fraud Detection Covers One-Way Interviews Only

VidCruiter takes the top rating because it writes down more of the trust picture than any rival. But its fraud detection watches only one format. Its AI Candidate Fraud Detection flags odd behavior and answers written by AI. It runs on recorded interviews only. That is the right place for the coverage to sit, since the recorded round is where nobody is watching.

Live interviews get a different set of tools: rating guides in the window, live transcripts, and recordings you can check back. That helps you score the same way each time. None of it proves who a person is. The judgment of whoever runs the call becomes the control at exactly the stage the fraud module stops watching.

  • Best for: high-volume one-way screening, with a human reviewing the shortlist afterwards.
  • Key features: video proctoring as a priced module, fraud and AI-answer detection on recorded responses, structured rating guides on live calls.
  • Watch for: no ID check or face match written down anywhere in the flow, and quote-only pricing.

Talview: Strong Identity Checks, Unclear One-Way Coverage

Talview documents deeper ID checks than anything ranked below it. Its software matches the candidate's selfie or ID scan against a baseline photo and third-party ID records before the session starts. It then checks that the same face stays on camera throughout, backed by voice checks and deepfake scans. On paper that is the strongest ID story in this lineup.

The gap is where those checks apply. Talview's own one-way interview pages mention no proctoring, no ID check and no face match. Score that as a gap in what they publish, not a low mark. Get one-way coverage in writing before you sign anything.

  • Best for: roles carrying code, customer data or admin access, where ID depth decides the purchase.
  • Key features: ID-to-selfie matching, ongoing face and voice checks, Secure Browser, live and recorded proctoring.
  • Watch for: pricing they do not publish, with a typical minimum commitment, and silence on async coverage.

HireVue: AI Scoring but No Identity Verification

HireVue asks near $35,000 a year and documents no ID capture, no face match, and no liveness or deepfake detection. Its AI reads transcripts and ranks candidates before a human reviews them. That ranking is a screening function. It is not an identity control. You end up with an audit-ready record of what every candidate said, and no proof of who said it.

That matters more here than anywhere else in the grid, because state AI hiring laws were written precisely about scored video assessment. You inherit the largest compliance surface in this comparison. You inherit none of the identity assurance.

  • Best for: structured, high-volume screening where scoring consistency matters more than verified identity.
  • Key features: AI transcript scoring, ranked shortlists, enterprise reporting.
  • Watch for: ranking runs before human review, so the bias audit and the consent notice fall to you.

Willo: One-Way Only, No AI Scoring of Video Answers

Willo runs one-way interviews only, which limits its exposure to a single failure mode. It also refuses to score video answers with AI at all. Algorithmic scoring stops at multiple-choice questions that have right and wrong answers. For a team wary of automated judgment on a recording, that restraint is the point.

Its fraud control, Real Talk, checks the answer rather than the person. It labels each response naturally spoken, potentially scripted, or potentially AI-generated. Treat those labels as prompts for a follow-up question, not verdicts, because a well-prepared candidate and a non-native speaker both read as scripted.

The identity claim is thinner. Willo Verified promises a certified report that the candidate is who they claim to be. Yet nothing published describes a face match, a liveness check, or the point in the flow where the check runs. That is why the grid records a claim rather than a control.

  • Best for: async screening where you want a compliance posture and no AI judging the video answer.
  • Key features: Real Talk answer-authenticity signals, one-way interviews, published anti-cheat guardrails.
  • Watch for: an identity product with no published mechanism behind it.

Interviewer.AI: Face Matching Is a Paid Add-On

Interviewer.AI is the only platform here that publishes a face-match score band. Candidates supply a government-issued ID. The system compares it to the live face. The result bands as low at 50 or below, medium above 50, and high above 80. That transparency matters, because you can set a threshold, write it into a hiring policy and defend the number later.

The catch is that the check is a purchased add-on, switched on for each interview rather than running by default. You make that decision role by role. The core product raises a separate duty. What you are buying is automated candidate ranking: resume scoring plus video scoring on an annual credit allocation. That places you squarely inside automated employment decision rules.

  • Best for: employers who want a numeric identity threshold they can document and enforce.
  • Key features: government-ID to live-face comparison, published score bands, per-interview toggle.
  • Watch for: ranking is the product, not a feature, so plan for the bias audit from the start.

Jobma: Proctoring Reports, Nothing Verifiable on Identity

Jobma gives you one trust record you can actually open and read. It is a report on each candidate. The report logs mobile-phone use, and it stamps the time of every moment the candidate shrinks the interview screen. For a hiring manager looking at a borderline recording, that log is proof.

On who the person is, the picture changes. Jobma's fraud page claims ID checks, selfie checks and liveness detection. Yet the same page names no ID firm and says Jobma does not spot or store face and voice data. Its own candidate walkthroughs cover desktop and mobile. Neither shows an ID capture, face match or liveness step anywhere in the interview flow. A check the candidate never meets cannot be scored as a control.

  • Best for: teams that want a proctoring log they can read, across both one-way and live interviews.
  • Key features: window-switch and mobile-use flags, a proctoring report per candidate, both interview formats.
  • Watch for: ID claims that the published candidate journey never puts to work.

Spark Hire: Validated AI Triage, No Identity Checks

Spark Hire can prove its AI scores answers accurately. Nothing in its documentation proves who recorded them. Spark Hire validated its AI Video Review on 68,236 candidate submissions, and against human ratings the scores reach over 90% accuracy. That is more evidence than most vendors in this comparison publish about anything.

Every dimension it scores is a performance signal, not an identity one. The ranking decides which recordings a human ever watches. A fraudulent submission that reads well can advance without anyone opening it. The only integrity-adjacent control on the product page is an employer-set retake limit.

  • Best for: validated triage at volume, with identity verified somewhere else in your process.
  • Key features: one-way and live interviews, evidence-backed AI scoring, employer-set retake limits.
  • Watch for: AI ranking runs before human review, so arrange the bias audit yourself.

Hireflix: No Candidate Login or Identity Verification

Hireflix closes this ranking at its floor, and it is honest about why. Candidates record answers with no application, no login and no registration. A submitted response binds to the invite link rather than to a verified person. Forward that link and you forward the interview.

Nothing in its published material describes proctoring, an ID check, a face match or deepfake detection. No axis in this rating can move Hireflix up, because every one of them begins with knowing who recorded the answer. It remains the right buy for one situation: fast, high-volume screening of roles with little system or data access. You verify identity at a later stage instead.

  • Best for: speed-first screening of low-access roles at the cheapest entry price in the set.
  • Key features: frictionless candidate experience, one-way interviews, no candidate account required.
  • Watch for: identity verification is entirely your job, at a later stage of the process.

Consent, Bias Audits and Retention Stay Your Duty

Every control in the grid answers one question: who recorded the answer. None of them answers for what you do with the recording afterwards. No certification on a vendor's security page transfers the legal duties of your selection process. The lawful basis for recording, the notice to candidates and the retention schedule all remain the employer's.

HireVue makes that point expensively. It agreed to a $3.75 million settlement. The deal won preliminary approval on June 25, 2026 2. The claim was that its software captured facial geometry and voiceprints from Illinois applicants without the written notice and consent Illinois' Biometric Information Privacy Act requires 2. The facial analysis behind a face match is exactly the processing that settlement priced. So the feature you buy for integrity is also the feature that carries the consent obligation.

Liability follows you even when somebody else built the filter. In Mobley v. Workday, the court let claims proceed on an agency theory. That means employers may answer for delegating candidate rejection to an automated tool. New York City goes further. Local Law 144 bars use of an automated employment decision tool unless an independent bias audit ran within the past year 3. The employer, not the developer, must arrange that audit, publish the results and notify candidates in advance 4.

How long each platform keeps the recording

A recorded interview does not disappear after the hire. It is video of a candidate's face and voice on a vendor's servers. When that candidate later asks what you hold on them, the request lands on your desk, not the vendor's. The retention clock deserves as much attention as the feature grid, and across these nine platforms it is set inconsistently.

HireVue's default retention runs up to three years. The hiring company sets that window, not HireVue and not the candidate. Talview archives every processed video, live and async alike, once it is 90 days old. Getting one back takes a support ticket and at least two business days, which is slow when a discrimination complaint carries a deadline. Jobma and Willo publish no retention period at all for recordings, transcripts or identity records.

Settle this in the contract, not in a sales call. Ask which vendor actually performs the identity check. Ask whether you receive the document image or only a pass-or-fail verdict. Ask how long those images are kept and who can retrieve them 2. Until the answers are written down, the retention schedule, like the consent notice and the bias audit, is yours alone to defend.

Match Verification Depth to the Role's Access Risk

The grid does not crown one winner. It hands you a verdict per constraint. The constraint is the access the role carries. Hire the wrong person into a low-access role and you lose hiring effort and a replacement search. Hire the wrong person into engineering, finance, cybersecurity or IT administration and you have handed credentials and system access to someone you never identified. Match verification depth to that difference and buy nothing past it.

If your hiring volume sits in one-way screening, VidCruiter puts its fraud detection exactly where the exposure is. Staff your live final rounds with trained interviewers, because the module stops watching there. If your open roles touch code, customer data or admin tools, Talview's identity depth is the reason to buy it. Pin down its one-way coverage before you sign. If a bad hire means a rehire rather than a breach, Hireflix and Spark Hire will screen quickly and leave identity to you.

For low-access roles, buy no integrity module at all. A camera-on policy, a government ID checked on a live call, and half a day of interviewer training cover the risk. That tier costs almost nothing beyond time. One free control outperforms most of them. Announce an in-person final round, and stand-in candidates tend to withdraw before it happens.
