# Video Interview Platform That Stops Deepfakes and Proxies

You are three screens into a Tuesday. The fourth candidate is the best of the morning: crisp answers, specific examples, and a video that stutters for half a second before every reply. You will probably move this person forward. Nothing in the recording tells you whether the face on screen belongs to the name on the application.

That gap is the subject here. A video interview platform records an answer beautifully. It cannot prove who gave it. Here are six of them, all competent at recording and scoring, judged on one question. What does each actually document about proving who is on camera?

Quick answer

Most video interview platforms do not verify identity at all. They analyse the answer rather than the applicant, so a fluent impostor passes without a murmur. Of the six here, only VidCruiter and Testlify document identity checks inside the assessment. Hireflix takes a selfie and stops there. Willo connects a third-party check if you buy one.

HireVue and Spark Hire expect the verifying to happen elsewhere, usually a background check or an in-person final round. None of that is wrong, as long as you chose it deliberately. Work out which roles could not survive the wrong person holding the login, and buy identity controls for those.

Deepfakes and proxy candidates are a documented hiring threat

A proxy candidate is what the name suggests. Someone else sits the interview and answers well. The person who turns up on the first Monday cannot do the work. The deepfake version is newer: a real face, borrowed and rendered live over the call, in a voice that was never in the room.

Video interview software records candidates answering a fixed set of questions. Some record on their own time, some live with an interviewer. The system transcribes and scores the answers so a panel can compare people fairly. Every part of that design assumes the face on the recording belongs to the applicant. Nothing in the recording proves it.

The scale is documented in court. The Department of Justice charged Christina Chapman with helping North Korean operatives use 68 stolen identities to get hired at 309 U.S. companies 1, 1. Sit with that number for a moment. Three hundred and nine hiring teams ran their process, liked what they saw, and issued credentials to a stranger.

It is not only nation-state operators. In Greenhouse research, 31% of recruiters said they had seen a different person interviewing from the one who applied 2. That is one recruiter in three. Ask your own team whether anyone has had that suspicion and could not prove it.

How we chose these video interview platforms

One rule decided this comparison. A control counts only if the vendor documents it. Marketing language about integrity was ignored, and so was every feature that scores an answer. Scoring a transcript tells you how somebody answered. It can never tell you who was speaking.

Ask a vendor how they know the face is real and you get one of four answers. The most confident is active liveness. The software asks the candidate to blink, smile or turn their head, and treats the response as proof of a live human. That defeats someone holding a photograph up to a webcam. It does nothing against an injected video feed, because the fake blinks on cue 3.

So the evidence we accepted was independent lab testing. CEN/TS 18099 covers virtual camera detection, and iBeta Level 3 covers spoofing resistance 4. A vendor pays a laboratory to run these and can hand you the report. Ask for it. If you get a features page instead, nobody has seriously attacked that control.

HireVue: enterprise assessment, no documented identity verification

HireVue is the platform most people picture when they hear the category name. Candidates record timed answers to standardised questions. The system transcribes them and scores them against an assessment framework, so a panel compares like with like. Essentials pricing starts at $35,000 a year 5.

What it does not do is check who is answering. HireVue documents no ID verification, no face matching, no liveness detection, no browser lockdown and no deepfake or proxy detection. Everything the AI judges comes out of the transcript. A capable stand-in earns a strong score, and that score is what your panel sees.

Check the compliance history before you sign. HireVue settled a class action for $3.75 million over collecting facial geometry and voiceprints from Illinois applicants without written consent 12. The lesson travels well beyond Illinois. Write down what biometric data your platform collects, tell candidates before they record, and keep the consent.

Spark Hire: async screening built for small teams

Spark Hire sits at the other end of the buying conversation. It costs $249 a month billed annually, with unlimited jobs and users 6. A two-person talent team can put it on a card and start screening this week.

Its AI reads answers, not applicants. Transcripts get scored against competencies like communication and execution, and the hiring manager gets a summary instead of forty minutes of video. That is useful. A summary of a fluent answer also looks identical whether the speaker is the candidate or their more articulate friend.

For a first round that takes a hundred applicants down to twelve, that is a fair trade. It holds only if you can name the stage where identity actually gets checked. Write that stage down. If nobody checks before the offer, the check does not exist yet.

Willo: European data residency, identity checks bolted on

Willo is built for asynchronous screening with a GDPR-first, Europe-hosted design 6. Your legal team may have spent six weeks deciding whether a US-hosted tool can hold recordings of German applicants. Willo keeps them in Europe by default, so the question never reaches the lawyers.

On integrity it does about half the job. Willo locks the candidate's screen and flags likely cheating during the recording. Identity itself depends on an optional third-party service rather than anything built in 7. A session can look clean by Willo's own measure and still be the wrong person.

Buy it when compliance and volume are the constraint and identity is handled elsewhere. In a lot of European hiring, it is. Do not buy it expecting screen lockdown to double as identity assurance. Those are different controls that happen to sit near each other in the settings.

VidCruiter: the most configurable option for regulated hiring

VidCruiter turns up where the hiring process is written into a regulation rather than a playbook. Think government agencies, school districts, and public bodies that evidence every step to an auditor. Pricing starts at $5,000 a year and bills per module by usage 8, so cost tracks how much you switch on.

Its proctoring module is the closest thing to identity work here outside Testlify. It verifies ID before the test starts, watches the browser for tab switching, blocks copy and paste, and records the session 9. That recording is what an auditor wants. Not a green tick, but the evidence behind it.

The price of that flexibility is time. Somebody has to do the configuring, and the panel needs training on the rating guides first. Choose VidCruiter when your process is dictated to you and has to survive review. Do not choose it because you want to be screening by Friday.

Hireflix: the one-way video interview without the login

Hireflix has one obsession, which is getting candidates to finish. There is no account to create and nothing to schedule. A link arrives, and the candidate records the one-way video interview on their phone whenever it suits them. It costs $75 to $150 a month depending on company size 10.

The identity step is a selfie with no prompts, known as passive liveness. The numbers explain the choice. Passive checks hold completion above 95% 13. Verification that asks for gestures or multi-step challenges drops completion to around 60% 13. Four applicants in ten walk away rather than blink at a camera on command.

That is a defensible call for a first round and an indefensible one for a final. You are buying completion with assurance. The trade works only if a later stage does the verifying. If Hireflix is the only camera your process points at a candidate, you are not screening for fraud.

Testlify: proctoring and identity verification inside the assessment

Testlify puts the identity work in the same place as the work being assessed. The candidate is verified before the assessment opens, and the session is monitored while they take it. Anything unusual comes back as a flagged session, with the video evidence and the activity log attached 11. Somebody on your team makes the call.

That sequence matters more than any single feature in it. When identity lives in one tool and the assessment in another, a reviewer has two timelines to reconcile. Most give up. When it is one record, the flag points at a timestamp. Five minutes instead of an afternoon.

It also protects the honest candidate, which is the part most fraud tooling forgets. A dropped connection, a flatmate crossing the room, a laptop that switches windows by itself. Each looks like a violation to a detector and like an ordinary Tuesday to a person. A human at the end gives those candidates a second look.

Match the controls to the role, not the budget

The mistake now is buying one level of rigour and applying it to everybody. The strongest frameworks run in tiers, switching controls on according to what the role can reach 14. A baseline layer runs on every assessment: browser lockdown, webcam capture, basic monitoring. Compliant candidates never notice it.

The second tier turns on where the assessment result effectively decides the hire. It adds real-time facial comparison and anomaly detection to the same session. The trigger is access, not seniority. A junior engineer with production credentials is a riskier hire than a director who approves budgets.

The top tier is a person. Flagged sessions go to human review, where somebody watches the recording and decides to pass, re-test or disqualify 14. Treat a flag as a question, not a verdict. An internet outage produces the same timing anomaly as a candidate reading from a second screen.

None of this has to start with a purchase. A camera-on policy, a photo ID check at the final round, and a short briefing on what a proxy interview feels like cost nothing. Spend real money where the answer to "what could this person reach on day one" makes you pause.

Scale your controls to role risk

Judge a video interview platform on what it documents, not on what it claims. The safest processes detect with software and decide with people, and they scale both to what the role can reach. Frequently asked questions

What is the best video interviewing platform?

There is no single winner. VidCruiter for regulated hiring, HireVue for structured assessment at scale, Testlify when the assessment itself decides the hire. Match the platform to the role's risk, not to your budget.

What is a proxy candidate?

Someone who sits the interview or test in place of the real applicant. The person you assessed is not the person you hired. You usually find out in their first week.

Can deepfakes pass a video interview?

Yes, against basic checks. A blink test proves nothing when the whole video feed is injected. Stopping one takes layered controls, plus a human reviewing whatever the system flags.

Do video interview platforms prevent cheating?

Most catch the obvious version with browser lockdown and webcam capture. Beyond that they vary enormously, and none of them prevent anything alone. Somebody still has to review what gets flagged.

When do you need identity verification in hiring?

Whenever the role reaches something you cannot hand to a stranger: production systems, customer data, money. First-round screening can skip it if a later stage does the checking.
