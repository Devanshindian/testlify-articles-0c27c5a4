# Skills Assessment Terms Explained From the Recruiter's Seat

You are running a skills assessment this week, and the vendor's page keeps saying validity, cut score, adverse impact. None of it tells you what changes on your screen. Guessing costs you: 44.5% of HR and hiring staff say nobody ever checks that their tests work at all.

Read one word wrong and you get a cut score you cannot defend, or a cheating flag that costs a good person the job. This page explains each term from the seat of the person running the test.

Quick answer

A skills assessment is a scored test every person sits the same way, so you can compare them. Validity asks whether the test measures what it claims; reliability asks whether the score would hold up if they sat it again. A cut score is a judgment call, not a fact in the data, and the four-fifths rule flags adverse impact when one group passes at less than 80% of the top group's rate. A cheating flag is evidence for a person to read, never an auto reject.

What Is a Skills Assessment, and Why Companies Use Them

A skills assessment is any scored test you give before you make an offer 1. Every person sits the same test, marked the same way. A resume records what someone claims; the test records what they can do.

That is why 56% of employers now use these tests to check what people know and can do 1. Of those, 78% say the hires that followed were better, SHRM found 1. You stop buying claims and start buying proof.

Types of Skills Assessment Tests

Every test sits on a scale of fidelity, meaning how closely it looks like the real job 2. A work sample or an assessment centre puts a person in job conditions and marks what they do. A paper test only asks whether they know the right move.

  • Skills tests: a piece of the real job, like a typing test for an admin role
  • Cognitive tests: reasoning, numbers, words, and how fast someone learns
  • Judgment tests (SJTs): a scene, say an angry customer, and which reply is right 2
  • Personality tests: whether someone will do the job, not whether they can 3
  • Structured interviews: the same questions live, marked against one rubric
  • Physical, drug and background checks: a fact, not a skill

Big reviews put cognitive tests among the strongest signs of who will do well, whatever the role 4.

How Employers Choose Which Test to Run

Before you shop, run a job analysis. Name how people most often fail in this role, and the two or three signals that would catch it 56. Skip it and you bolt one model onto every role, and nobody trusts the result.

No single test carries the whole picture. Mixing methods beats picking one, as Schmidt and Hunter showed in 1998 10. Run the cheap test first to cut the pool, then keep the work sample for whoever is left.

Validity and Reliability: What Validated Really Means

Validity just means a test measures what it claims 11. When a vendor says validated, ask which of three kinds they mean, because the three do not promise the same thing 11.

Construct validity asks whether the test measures the trait it names, like how someone thinks. Content validity asks whether the questions match the work the job needs. That is the kind with legal weight: the Uniform Guidelines and the EEOC require a test to fit the job it screens for. A sales trait test given to a coder fails, unless the coder has to sell.

Criterion validity asks whether high scorers do better on the job, judged by a boss's rating. Only this kind closes the gap for a hiring call 12. Score people when they apply and check them a year later: that is predictive 12. Score staff whose work you already know and that is concurrent, faster but weaker 1213.

Reliability is the other half. Sit the same test twice and a steady test hands back nearly the same number 14. Below about.70, do not lean on it 15. And a study run only on people already hired, a range-restricted sample, reports a smaller link to job performance than the real one 16.

The Norm Group Decides What a Percentile Means

A 70th percentile score does not mean someone got 70% of the questions right 17. On a hard test, that person may have got fewer than 60% right 17. Read the rank as a percentage and you reject people who cleared the bar 17.

Norming turns a raw score, the count of right answers, into a spot within a reference group 1819. That group is the norm group, and groups differ: all working adults, graduates, or only people who already do the job. A coder at the 60th percentile against all adults can fall to the 40th against other coders 17. Match the norm group to the role before anyone starts testing 17.

How to Set a Cut Score

A cut score is the point that splits who moves on from who does not 20. It also goes by cut-off, cutpoint, and pass mark 20. One mark below and a person is out, on work that was nearly the same 20.

That point is a judgment, not a fact hiding in the data 20. No test holds a natural line between good enough and not; people draw it on purpose 20. Raise the cut and those who pass are stronger, but fewer are left 21. Lower it and you get more people and more risk, by choice or by default 21.

The formal way to place the line is a standard-setting study, where experts decide what counts as a pass 2223. The usual method is Angoff: each expert rates every question on how many just-good-enough people would get it right 20.

Many tests carry more than one cut 20. Under a compensatory rule, one cut applies to the combined score, so a strong area covers a weak one 20. Under a conjunctive rule, they must clear each test on its own 20.

How to Calculate Adverse Impact With the Four-Fifths Rule

Adverse impact is what a test produces when it screens out one protected group at a much higher rate than another 2425. It is about the outcome, not the intent: a cut score can do this with nobody meaning it to 26.

The four-fifths rule, also called the 80% rule, puts a line on it. Divide the people hired from a group by everyone who applied from it, and you get that group's selection rate 24. Below 80% of the top group's rate, the ratio flags adverse impact and calls for a closer look 24.

A company screens 100 white applicants and hires 50, a 50% rate. It screens 50 Black applicants and hires 15, a 30% rate. Divide 30 by 50 and you get 60%, well under the line, so the test shows adverse impact against Black applicants 24.

A failed ratio is not a verdict. You then have to show the test is job-related and consistent with business necessity, a phrase from Griggs v. Duke Power in 1971 2728. The rules sit in the Uniform Guidelines, adopted by the EEOC in 1978 2429.

Is Your Screening Stack an AEDT Under NYC Local Law 144?

An automated employment decision tool (AEDT) is any computer process built on machine learning, stats, or AI. It turns applicant data into a score, rank, or piece of advice used to help or replace a hiring call 30. New York City wrote that into Local Law 144, in force since July 5, 2023 31. If a tool does more than sort information, and its output shapes who moves on, it likely counts 32.

That covers tools most recruiters already run:

  • Resume screeners scored on which past hires did well
  • Video interview tools that score words, pace, or how someone speaks
  • Thinking and personality tests whose number feeds your hiring system on its own 30

A bias audit is an outside, neutral test of whether an AEDT could hurt someone's odds by race, ethnic group, or sex 33. It runs once a year, with results posted in public and people told at least 10 working days ahead 32. Miss either step and the city can fine you $500 to $1,500 each time 33. The law only makes you show the numbers; it never sets a pass mark 34.

Reasonable Accommodation and Essential Functions

A reasonable accommodation is a change to how you run a test, not to what the test measures. The Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) wants scores to show what someone can do, not what their condition blocks 35. In practice that means extra time, a reader, or a screen-reader format.

Essential functions are the core duties of a job, not the side ones, and that is what you may hold fixed 36. Draw the line in the job analysis, before you pick a test. Nobody has to say disability or ADA to make a request 37. Treating a rough one as no request is how employers land in trouble 38.

A Proctoring Flag Is Evidence, Not a Verdict

A proctored assessment is a secure test watched by a person, by software, or both 39. The point is proof: if someone can quietly paste an answer from a chatbot, the score tells you nothing 39. It runs in three modes, each trading cost against goodwill 40.

ModeHow it runsThe trade
LiveA person watches the webcam and can stop the test 40Feels safest, costs most, still open to human error 40
RecordedThe session is taped and reviewed later 40Less intrusive, but judged with no context 40
SoftwareIt flags what looks odd for a person to read 40Scales furthest, throws the most false flags 40

A flag is evidence for a person to read, never an auto reject 39. A false positive is a flag raised by something that only looked like cheating 41. Think of a glance away to think, or a family member walking past. So use a mix: software catches at scale, a person makes the final call 42.

Putting the Vocabulary to Work

Knowing these words does not remove the judgment calls behind a cut score, a validity claim, or a cheating flag. What changes is that you can read a vendor's page, question a number, and defend the call you make. Frequently asked questions

What is a skills assessment?

A scored test that has every person do the same task, so you can compare them directly 1. It shows what someone can do today, like a typing test for an admin role.

What is involved in a skills assessment?

A job analysis to name the core tasks, a test matched to them, a norm group to read the score, and often proctoring to confirm the result is real 51839.

Can you give me an example of a skills assessment?

A typing test for an admin role, a coding test for a developer, or a case study for a specialist 9. Each has the person do a piece of the real job.

How much do skills assessments cost?

Most vendors charge per person tested, not per seat. Costs run from a few dollars to over $50 per test, based on how hard the job is and how many you run.
