# AI Recruitment Tools Ranked by Bias-Audit Evidence

Sign off on AI recruitment tools and your name goes behind a claim the vendor cannot prove: that the tool cuts bias. New York City's Local Law 144 says an outside firm must test these tools before you use one on a New York job seeker. Two of the five platforms here have no bias-audit record at all. One of them, Workday, is fighting a live lawsuit with nothing to set against it.

Quick answer

None of the five has a clean record. Pymetrics ranks first: an outside team found no group screened out at a far higher rate, with one gap left open. Eightfold AI is second, with full Local Law 144 results it says do not prove the model is bias-free; HireVue is third with two audits it will not show you, iCIMS fourth with no public audit, and Workday last, in court. A pass only proves what got tested, so ask before you sign.

How We Ranked These AI Hiring Tools

All five were scored on the same five things.

  • Outside bias audit: has an outside firm tested the tool, or is it just the vendor's word?
  • AEDT status: does it count as an AEDT (a hiring tool that scores or ranks people for you) under Local Law 144?
  • Four-fifths rule results: are the pass rates by race and sex made public?
  • Legal risk: is anyone suing, or has a watchdog been asked to look?
  • Scoring openness: does the vendor say what the score weighs?

Every score traces to a vendor's own words, an outside test, or a court file. Where nothing exists, that is a gap, not a pass.

1. Pymetrics: The Cleanest Bias Audit, and Its One Gap

Pymetrics sells 12 games built on brain-science tests that score traits like risk appetite and learning speed. It has sold the product as "entirely bias free."

That claim is why the audit counts. Pymetrics gave outside researchers full access, on a grant through Northeastern University, paid up front. They read the source code by hand and kept the right to publish what they found.

Every group's pass rate reached at least 80% of the top group's, and no race or sex data went into training. One gap remains. Pymetrics reuses a score for up to 330 days, so a score from one firm can resurface at another with no fresh test.

2. Eightfold AI: The NYC Local Law 144 Audit It Published in Full

BABL AI ran Eightfold's Local Law 144 audit with its own Criterion Audit Framework, a method based on financial audit practice 1.

The audit covered more than 29 million scored job seekers, from January 2024 to December 2025 2. The race and sex data came from what people chose to share 2. Eightfold published the whole thing, not a summary 3. It split results by sex, by the seven race groups the law names, and by every mix 4.

Eightfold still will not call it a clean bill of health. It says the audit does not prove the model is bias-free, and that no audit can.

3. HireVue: The Most Audited Tool, and the Least Open

HireVue films answers and boils gestures, pose, tone, pace and word choice down into one score. Its own top scientist told the Washington Post that face movements alone can make up 29% of it 5. Tools that read faces, tone or word choice fall under Local Law 144 6.

In 2019 the privacy group EPIC took HireVue to the Federal Trade Commission 7. The complaint said HireVue sold the tool as bias-free with no basis.

Two audits by O'Neil Risk Consulting followed in 2021, and neither was released in full 8. You can read a summary only after handing over your details and promising not to copy it 8. HireVue still will not show a person their own score 8. No public audit with pass-rate numbers exists for its video scoring.

4. iCIMS Under NYC Local Law 144: No Public Audit Record

iCIMS has no outside audit summary, no EEOC finding and no lawsuit on public view, in either direction.

The law does not attach to iCIMS as a firm. It attaches to the features you switch on. In a November 2024 blog post, iCIMS said only one Talent Cloud AI tool counts as an AEDT: Candidate Ranking using Role Fit 9. Sourcing and chatbots did not, so coverage turns on what you turned on 9.

iCIMS says it paid for outside audits of that feature in 2022 and 2023, both with good results 9. It hands the summary to buyers who ask and has not published one 9. An unpublished audit is not a proven pass.

5. Workday: No Bias Audit, and the Most Legal Risk

No bias-audit results, no Local Law 144 summary and no four-fifths numbers exist for Workday's AI hiring features 10. Its own site still sells "unbiased, AI-driven candidate grading" without saying what the model weighs 1112.

A court ruling cuts against you too. On May 28, 2026, a judge held that bias-test data for its Candidate Skills Match tool can stay hidden under lawyer-client privilege 13. The judge agreed the data was proof of possible bias, and let it stay hidden anyway 13.

An outside firm did run Local Law 144's math on HiredScore Spotlight in March 2026 14. Workday's own page says that study is not meant to meet any buyer's legal duty 14.

What Mobley v. Workday Changed for AI Hiring Tools

Derek Mobley, a Black man over 40 with a disability, applied for more than 100 jobs through firms using Workday's screening tool 15. He says he was turned down every time, often with no person reading the file 15. He argues Workday is liable as an "agent" of those firms, and that its picks hit people harder by race, age and disability 1516.

The court let that theory stand. In July 2024 it threw out Workday's claim that it merely carries out what employers set 17. In May 2025, Judge Rita Lin let the case run as a group claim under the federal age-bias law 18. It is open to everyone 40 and over turned down since September 2020 18.

No court has ruled on whether the tool really did that 19. What is settled is narrower and still large: the vendor, not only the employer, can be named 20.

What NYC Local Law 144 Asks of You, and What It Costs

Local Law 144 does not ban AI in hiring. It puts three duties on you before you use an AEDT 21. An outside firm must run the bias audit, and it cannot be older than a year. You post the results in public, and you tell the job seeker at least 10 business days out.

What counts as an AEDT turns on what it does, not what it is called 22. If it scores, ranks or picks people, it is in scope; plain storage and keyword search are not 22.

Fines run from $500 to $1,500, and each day and each missing item counts on its own 2324. Yet the State Comptroller found the city agency missed about 17 of every 18 breaches 22. Weak enforcement is not low risk: private suits need no agency 25.

The Four-Fifths Rule, and How to Run It

The four-fifths rule, also called the 80% rule, is the federal test for adverse impact: one group screened out at a far higher rate 2627. Anything under 0.80 is a flag 28.

Find each group's pass rate, meaning hires divided by people who applied. Take the highest as the mark, divide every other rate by it, then compare to 0.80 28. Below, 135 people sit the same written test 28. The pool passes on sex and fails on race in the same round 29.

What a Passing Bias Audit Doesn't Prove

A clean Local Law 144 result is not a fairness stamp. The city's own guidance says the audit reports set numbers and does not prove the tool is fair in every job. The law sets no pass or fail line at all; it asks for disclosure, not a result 3233.

An audit covers only the tool as it stood on the day it ran 34. A vendor can change the model, the training data or the score cut-off without setting off a new one 34.

A pass says nothing about whether the tool picks good people either. As one review of a friendly audit put it, a tool can clear the bar and still not find the best qualified women 35.

What to Ask a Vendor Before You Sign

You carry the risk, not the vendor: buying an outside tool never moves the duty off the firm using it 36. The EEOC pulled its 2023 guidance in January 2025, but Title VII did not change, and people can still sue 3725. So do the work before you sign 38:

  • A reason per score: ask for a plain-words "why this person ranked here", not just a number 39.
  • Controls you can tune: set weights and cut-offs per role, each setup written down and checked on its own 39.
  • Proof behind the numbers: if a vendor claims 95%, ask what it was measured against: recruiter clicks, real hires, or a labelled set 39.

Then keep a person in the loop. A reviewer who waves through a pick in under 60 seconds is not judging anything, and a court will look at that 40.

Frequently asked questions

Which AI tool is best for recruitment?

No one tool wins. Pymetrics has the fullest public audit proof. Workday is being sued, and Eightfold says its own audit does not prove the model is bias-free. Match the tool to your risk.

How to use AI tools for recruitment?

Read a vendor's published audit before you sign, then keep a trained person reviewing every score instead of letting the tool reject on its own 41. Renew the audit each year 42.

Does NYC Local Law 144 apply if my company isn't based in New York?

Yes, if the person lives in one of the five boroughs. What counts is where they live, not where you are based, even for a remote job 2243.

Is an ATS the same thing as an AI recruitment tool?

No. An ATS (applicant tracking system) runs the hiring workflow. An AI hiring tool adds scoring or ranking inside it. Workday and iCIMS build that scoring into their ATS.

What's the difference between AI recruitment tools and skills-based assessments?

AI recruitment tools score resumes, video or chat to rank people. Skills tests check them on real job tasks against a fixed rubric, which gives proof that is easier to defend.
